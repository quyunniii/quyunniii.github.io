About

I am a fourth-year PhD student in the Department of Computer Science at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where I received an en-route M.S. in 2025 and expect to graduate from CS Ph.D. program in summer 2027. My tentative thesis title is Towards Inference-Time Reliability & Acquisition Utility.

My research develops machine learning methods for two problems: choosing which features to acquire over time when every measurement has a cost, and making reliable predictions on inputs unlike the training data. I apply them to healthcare prognosis, longitudinal psychological assessment, and virtual screening in drug discovery.

Before UNC, I completed an Honours B.S. in Computer Science and Data Science with High Distinction at the University of Toronto (2023), where I worked on neural-network uncertainty and medical image segmentation and classification.

Research

Longitudinal active feature acquisition

In many settings every measurement has a cost: a clinical test, a questionnaire item, a follow-up visit. I develop methods that choose which features to acquire at each timepoint so that predictions stay accurate while the burden on patients and study participants falls. This includes differentiable acquisition policies for context and temporal features (REACT), nonmyopic cost-tradeoff planning (CAPE), and distilling learned policies into interpretable decision trees.

Applications: healthcare prognosis, longitudinal psychological assessment

Reliable out-of-distribution prediction

In virtual screening, the most promising new chemical scaffolds are often exactly the ones a model has never seen. I work on methods that extrapolate with calibrated confidence rather than rejecting novel inputs outright, so that useful candidates are ranked rather than discarded (EXPLOR). More recently, I'm working on multi-round OOD active learning for highly imbalanced chemical datasets.

Applications: virtual screening in drug discovery, highly imbalanced data

Publications

  1. Contrastive Nonmyopic Objective Cost-Tradeoff Acquisition for Longitudinal Data
    Dzung Dinh*, Boqi Chen*, Yunni Qu, Marc Niethammer, Junier Oliva
    NeurIPS 2026, main track

    CAPE plans feature acquisitions over time by scoring candidate plans with a nonmyopic objective that trades expected loss against cost, then retrieves precomputed plans at inference time from a learned embedding of partially observed data.

    OpenReviewNeurIPS page
  2. Relaxed Efficient Acquisition of Context and Temporal Features
    Yunni Qu, Dzung Dinh, Grant King, Whitney Ringwald, Bing Cai Kok, Kathleen Gates, Aidan Wright, Junier Oliva
    ACM BCB 2026 · long oral

    REACT is a differentiable framework that jointly chooses which onboarding descriptors to collect and which longitudinal measurements to take over time under cost constraints, improving prediction at lower acquisition cost on real health and behavioral data.

    ACM DLarXiv
  3. Reliable OOD Virtual Screening with Extrapolatory Pseudo-Label Matching
    Yunni Qu, Bhargav Vaduri, Karthikeya Jatoth, James Wellnitz, Dzung Dinh, Seth Veenbaas, Jonathan Chapman, Alexander Tropsha, Junier Oliva
    ACM BCB 2026 · long oral

    EXPLOR uses extrapolative pseudo-labeling on latent-space augmentations with a multi-headed network and a per-head matching loss, giving well-calibrated confidence on novel chemical scaffolds using only labeled training data.

    ACM DLarXiv
  4. Interpretable Active Feature Acquisition for Cost-Efficient Temporal Prediction with Reduced Participant Burden
    Yunni Qu*, Bing Cai Kok*, Whitney Ringwald, Grant King, Aidan Wright, Kathleen Gates, Junier Oliva
    Preprint, arXiv:2610.07452

    Selects a changing subset of questionnaire items to collect at each timepoint of a psychology study, and distills the learned acquisition policy into interpretable decision trees so that participant burden drops without losing forecasting accuracy.

    arXiv
  5. Neural Network Decision-Making Criteria Consistency Analysis via Inputs Sensitivity
    Eric Xing, Liangliang Liu, Xin Xing, Yunni Qu, Nathan Jacobs, Gongbo Liang
    ICPR 2022

    Networks trained repeatedly on the same task reach similar accuracy but can rely on very different input features; this work measures that inconsistency with feature-attribution maps and clustering analysis.

    IEEE Xplore
  6. Beware the Black-Box of Medical Image Generation: An Uncertainty Analysis by the Learned Feature Space
    Yunni Qu, David Yan, Eric Xing, Fengbo Zheng, Jie Zhang, Liangliang Liu, Gongbo Liang
    IEEE EMBC 2022

    U-Nets for medical image generation give stable output quality across training runs, yet their learned feature spaces differ substantially; this work analyzes that uncertainty with computational and clustering methods.

    IEEE Xplore
